# CaseFile evaluation report

How well CaseFile retrieves evidence and answers multi-step questions with citations, measured on a fixed,
reproducible question set: what works, where it fails, why, and how to improve it.

> **Status:** final. All runs, including the post-fix test run (section 5.4), are complete.

## 1. Setup

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Corpus | HotpotQA dev set (distractor setting): 66,555 Wikipedia paragraphs → 78,730 chunks in Qdrant Cloud, plus 10 conflicting documents added for the contradiction test (66,565 documents) |
| Embeddings | `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` (384 dimensions), local via fastembed |
| Retrieval | dense, BM25 (own encoder, Qdrant IDF), Reciprocal Rank Fusion (own), optional reranker (own: query coverage + MMR) |
| LLM | `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` (free tier), temperature 0, tool calling |
| Agent | multi-step loop, max 5 tool rounds, JSON answer with chunk-id citations, citation validator, abstention, conflict reporting |
| Default search mode | `hybrid` (chosen by tuning, section 4) |

## 2. The question set
`eval/eval_set.jsonl` + `eval/contradictory_set.jsonl`: **60 questions**, seeded (`EVAL_SEED = 2026`), so rebuilding
gives byte-identical files. Each category is split in half: **dev** (for decisions and tuning) and **test** (for the
final numbers).

| Category | Count | Source | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| bridge | 20 | HotpotQA | multi-hop: find a fact, then use it to find the next |
| comparison | 10 | HotpotQA | multi-hop: compare two named entities |
| simple | 10 | generated by Gemini from one paragraph each, then **checked by hand** (`eval/question_review.md`: 3 kept, 6 fixed, 1 replaced) | single-paragraph retrieval |
| unanswerable | 10 | hand-written: future events, fictional entities, false premises, out of scope; confirmed absent by search | abstention |
| contradictory | 10 | hand-written conflicting "sources" (each alters one year), validated and uploaded through the real pipeline; both sources retrieved for 10/10 questions | conflict reporting |

The 5 spot-check questions used earlier are excluded. One contradiction (The Horror Zine: founded 2006 vs first
published 2009) is kept as a deliberate **soft conflict**, since both statements can be true.

## 3. Metrics
- **Retrieval** (no LLM; ranked by document, a multi-chunk paragraph counts once):
  - **Recall@K**: share of supporting paragraphs in the top K.
  - **all-found@K**: every supporting paragraph in the top K. Multi-hop questions need both.
  - **MRR**: mean of 1/rank of the first supporting paragraph.
- **Answers:**
  - **Exact match** and **token F1** after HotpotQA's official normalization.
  - **LLM-judge correctness**: does the answer mean the same as the gold answer? The judge was checked in
    `eval/results/judge_check.md`.
  - **Evidence retrieved**: did the agent see every supporting paragraph?
  - **Citation precision/recall**: of the documents cited, how many support the answer, and how many of the supporting
    documents were cited.
- **Honesty:**
  - **Abstention recall**: refused on unanswerable questions.
  - **Abstention precision**: share of refusals that were justified. Contradictory questions are excluded, because
    declining there is valid.
  - **False-abstention rate**: refusals on answerable questions.
  - **Conflict detection**: share of contradictory questions where a conflict was reported.
- **Sample size:** each split has 20 answerable questions, so **one question = 0.05**. Differences of one question
  are noise.

## 4. Retrieval results

### 4.1 Search modes
| Split | Mode | R@5 | R@10 | all-found@5 | all-found@10 | MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dev | dense | 0.800 | 0.800 | 0.700 | 0.700 | 0.860 |
| dev | **hybrid** | 0.800 | **0.925** | 0.650 | **0.850** | **0.917** |
| dev | hybrid_rerank | 0.800 | 0.875 | 0.600 | 0.750 | 0.879 |
| test | dense | 0.775 | 0.825 | 0.700 | 0.800 | **0.825** |
| test | **hybrid** | **0.825** | **0.850** | **0.800** | 0.800 | 0.747 |
| test | hybrid_rerank | 0.825 | 0.825 | 0.800 | 0.800 | 0.767 |

- **Dev:** hybrid is best on all-found@10 and MRR.
- **Test:** hybrid gets both supporting paragraphs into the top 5 more often (0.80 vs 0.70), but dense ranks the
  *first* hit higher (MRR 0.825 vs 0.747), mostly on comparison questions. There, BM25 raises paragraphs that merely
  mention the same names.
- Most test differences are 1–2 questions. **Conclusion:** hybrid is at least as good as dense for multi-hop
  retrieval, not clearly better on test.

### 4.2 Reranker tuning (dev only; `eval/results/tuning_notes.md`)
A grid of 60 combinations (`COVERAGE_WEIGHT` × `MMR_LAMBDA` × `DENSE_RANK_LIMIT`). The metric was fixed before
running: all-found@10, then MRR.
- **Best:** coverage 0, λ 1.0 gives 0.85 / 0.917, vs plain hybrid 0.80 / 0.867. That is one question better, i.e.
  noise. These settings turn the reranker's features off ("hybrid in disguise").
- **Isolating the features:** MMR diversity costs 2 questions (0.85 → 0.75); exact-word coverage costs 1.
- **Decision:** plain hybrid is the default; the reranker remains available as `hybrid_rerank`. On test the reranker
  again doesn't beat hybrid (identical all-found, MRR within noise).

### 4.3 Chunking strategies (`eval/results/chunking_comparison.json`)
Fixed vs sentence chunking at 800 and 300 characters, on the same seeded sample (5,070 paragraphs), 40 questions,
hybrid search.
- At 800 characters, the two strategies are **identical** (R@5 0.938, all-found@10 0.875, MRR 0.947): HotpotQA
  paragraphs are short (median 474 characters), so about 85% are one chunk either way.
- At 300 characters they differ by at most one question, in both directions, for 2–2.5× as many chunks.
- **Kept:** sentence chunking at 800 characters.
- Scores are higher than on the full corpus because fewer paragraphs compete; only the comparison is meaningful.

### 5.3 What the first test run revealed
Of the agent's 7 misses on answerable test questions:
- **2 were caused by a bug.** The model cited a **document id** where a **chunk id** was required. Search results
  showed both, so the citation validator correctly removed the citation, and the correct answer ("Spurs",
  "filmmaker") became an abstention.
- **The bug also occurred on dev** (1 answer, the "writer" question), where it had been taken for an ordinary false
  abstention. So it's a genuine defect, not something found by tuning on test.
- The baseline never had it: its passages don't include a document id.
- **Fixes:**
  - The tool description and answer format now say which id to cite.
  - The validator resolves a document-id citation to that document's *retrieved* chunks; never-retrieved documents
    stay invalid.
  - One retry on an empty or invalid final reply.
- On the next dev run, the clearer instruction alone prevented document-id citations: the validator resolved 0 and the
  retry repaired 0. Those two remain as safety nets.

The first test run (5.2) stays reported unchanged. This run is labelled as post-fix because the bug was diagnosed
while analysing test results.

**What changed compared with 5.2:**
- **The agent now beats the baseline on test.** Judge accuracy went 0.65 → 0.85 and false abstentions 0.30 → 0.05.
  The gain is on bridge questions (0.60 → 0.80), where the agent's second search finds a paragraph the single search
  misses (bridge evidence retrieved 0.80 vs 0.60).
- **No document-id citations** were made: the validator resolved 0 and the repair retry was not needed, the same as on
  dev. The clearer instruction did the work.
- **The baseline also moved (0.70 → 0.75)** without any change to its code. That is run-to-run variance (6.8): one
  question is 0.05.
- **Only 3 of the agent's 20 answerable answers are wrong:**
  - "Which year was the voice actress … born?": answered 1968, gold 1967. The supporting paragraph was never
    retrieved, so the agent took the year from a different actress (bridge hop, 6.1).
  - "Which Grammy-nominated album … John Bowman?": abstained. Evidence not retrieved (6.1).
  - "Are Leopold Lummerstorfer and Valeria Sarmiento both authors?": answered "yes", gold "no". Both paragraphs were
    retrieved; the model read "wrote films" as "author" (6.4).
- **Exact match is lower than the baseline (0.55 vs 0.70) even though accuracy is higher.** All 6 extra misses are
  correct answers in a different form: "6" vs "six", "McLean" vs "McLean, Virginia", "Todd Field" vs "William Todd
  Field", "director" vs "filmmaker", "Charles Nungesser" vs "Charles Eugène", "The Walt Disney Company" vs "contract
  with The Walt Disney Company". The judge marked all 6 correct (6.6).

## 6. Error analysis: where it fails, why, and how to improve

### 6.1 Bridge questions: the second hop
- **What:** the second supporting paragraph is about an entity the question never names ("the woman who portrayed
  Corliss Archer" leads to *Shirley Temple*). A single search can't target it.
- **Evidence:**
  - In the milestone-4 spot check, single search found the second paragraph in 0/3 bridge questions; the agent found
    and cited it in 3/3.
  - On the test split, bridge evidence retrieval is 0.80 after the fixes (baseline 0.60). The 2 remaining bridge
    misses are both questions where the agent stopped searching or never discovered the bridge entity (5.4).
- **Improve:** prompt an explicit plan ("first find X, then search for what X leads to"); check after each search
  whether every entity the answer depends on has been looked up; allow one more search round when the evidence is
  incomplete.

### 6.2 Answer format vs exact match
- **What:** correct answers written as sentences ("The Genesis Suite was written in 1945…", "Yes, both are
  American.") scored 0.10 exact match against short gold answers.
- **Why:** the answer instruction ("short, direct answer") was too vague, and the yes/no rule correctly gives 0 to
  "Yes, both…".
- **Fix:** the answer field holds only the answer, with examples. Dev exact match went from 0.10 to 0.80 with no other
  change.

### 6.3 Citations that use the wrong id
See 5.3. The general lesson: when a tool returns several ids, the model will sometimes use the wrong one. Tell it
which id to cite, and validate citations rather than trusting them.

### 6.4 Comparison questions
- **What:** the weakest category on test: agent judge accuracy 0.40.
- **Failures:**
  - **Reasoning:** "Are Leopold Lummerstorfer and Valeria Sarmiento both authors?" → "yes" (gold: no; both are film
    directors).
  - **False abstentions:** the citation bug, now fixed.
- **Retrieval:** on comparisons, hybrid's keyword half can rank a paragraph that merely mentions the same names above
  the right one (test MRR: hybrid 0.767 vs dense 1.000 on comparison questions).
- **Improve:** for yes/no comparisons, require one claim per entity before answering; weight dense search higher for
  questions that name two entities.

### 6.5 The reranker
- **What:** tuning showed the reranker's features **hurt** multi-hop retrieval.
- **Why:**
  - MMR's diversity penalty pushes out a second needed paragraph that resembles the first (−2 questions on dev).
  - Exact-word coverage penalizes paraphrases. In milestone 4, the correct "Ed Wood" paragraph ("Edward Davis Wood
    Jr.") fell below an irrelevant "Woodson, Arkansas".
- **Improve:** a cross-encoder reranker that scores relevance by meaning, not word overlap.

### 6.6 Metrics and gold answers
- **Exact match is brittle:** "6" vs "six" scores 0 on both exact match and F1; "31 May 1980" vs "May 31, 1980" fails
  exact match.
- **Some HotpotQA gold answers are noisy:** "Charles Eugène" (a truncated full name), "contract with The Walt Disney
  Company", "filmmaker." with a trailing period.
- **This is why the LLM judge is reported.** Our check agreed with it 17/17 on non-exact answers and found it slightly
  lenient (`eval/results/judge_check.md`).
- **Improve:** normalize numbers ("six" ↔ "6") and dates before exact match.

### 6.7 Conflicts and abstention
- **Conflicts:** conflicts were reported 5/5 on dev and 5/5 on test.
  - The agent either abstains and explains the disagreement, or answers with the disagreement spelled out.
  - The **soft conflict** (founded vs first published) was also flagged, which is mild **over-reporting**.
- **Unanswerable questions:** all were refused, including the false-premise ones, where real paragraphs exist about
  the people involved.
- **Improve:** ask the model to distinguish "contradicts" from "both can be true" before reporting a conflict.

### 6.8 Run-to-run variance and sample size
- **Variance:** at temperature 0 the model still varies between runs. Dev exact match moved 0.80 → 0.70 between two
  runs while judge accuracy rose to 1.00, because the agent searched and phrased differently after a prompt change.
- **Sample size:** with 20 answerable questions per split, a single question moves a metric by 0.05.
- **Improve:** repeat runs (the LLM cache makes reruns of unchanged requests free) and a larger question set. The free
  tier's daily limit (500 requests) is the practical constraint.

### 6.9 Scope limits
- **Language:** Georgian documents barely work. The BM25 tokenizer drops non-Latin letters, and the embedding model is
  English-only (milestone 6). Fix: a Unicode tokenizer plus a multilingual embedding model; both need a re-index.
- **Free-tier constraints:**
  - 15 requests/minute (handled by a rate limiter and retry-after hints).
  - 500 requests/day: when the provider asks for a long wait, the system now fails fast with a clear error instead of
    retrying for minutes.
  - Model choice: `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, because larger models were overloaded (166–245 s per call).
